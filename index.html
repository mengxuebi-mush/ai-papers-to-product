和光谱的关系

有标准答案的任务可以用对错来训练和评估，没有标准答案的才需要人或 AI 的偏好。训练和评估遵循同一套逻辑，也继承同样的风险：裁判有偏差，模型就学会那个偏差。已有研究 后来的 DPO、RLAIF、可验证奖励都是在替换这条流水线里的某一块。

三种 loop，只有一种需要反思

类型循环的是什么需要反思吗主要风险
逐个处理一批不同的对象，每个做一次（如改表格每一行的字体）不需要做一半就停：τ-bench 里占失败的 19%（§5.2 原文），需要覆盖率检查
重试到通过同一件事反复做，直到评估器说成功不一定评估器判错，就会停在错误答案上
带反思的重试同一件事，每次失败后写下教训再来（Reflexion）需要错的教训被记住；卡在局部最优

已有研究 Loop engineering 通常被描述为 Goal → Action → Observation → Adjustment 的循环，再加上持久记忆（IBM 称为 spine），和 Reflexion 的结构几乎一一对应。

把论文叠在一起看

共同的盲区

每条的事实部分都来自原文，最后一句“对产品意味着什么”才是推测。

都用 LLM 代替人

原文 τ-bench 用 gpt-4 模拟用户；MT-Bench 用 GPT-4 当裁判；InstructGPT 用 reward model 代替标注员；Reflexion 在部分任务里用 LLM 或自写测试当 Evaluator。

推测 都没有拿 enterprise 领域专家验证过。

“人类”是一个很窄的群体

原文 MT-Bench 是研究生和众包用户；InstructGPT 是约 40 名主要在美国或东南亚的标注员；τ-bench 没有报告 human baseline。

推测 和他们一致，不等于和 SRE、admin 一致。

人和人本身就会分歧

原文 MT-Bench 专家之间一致率 81%；InstructGPT 标注员之间约 73–77%。分歧在训练和评分时被平均掉。

推测 少数用户群的偏好最容易在平均中消失。

只看结果，不看过程

原文 τ-bench 作者写明 r=1 是必要不充分条件，没经确认就执行也算成功；MT-Bench 只评最终回答。

推测 enterprise 需要知道谁批准的、agent 看到了什么。

失败没有轻重之分

原文 τ-bench 只有 0 和 1。按规则该拒绝时拒绝会得分，但任务做得到时“转人工”和“做错了”同样是 0。

推测 区分不了能被发现的失败和 silent failure。

偏好不等于正确

原文 MT-Bench 裁判偏爱更长的回答，会被错误数学答案带偏；Reflexion 自测题会让错误代码通过（Rust 16%）。

推测 写得漂亮但结论错的 RCA 可能得高分。

评估和训练会被反向优化

原文 InstructGPT 专门加 KL penalty 防止钻 reward model 的空子。

已有研究 reward hacking 是公认问题；RLHF 模型普遍有 sycophancy 倾向（Anthropic 2023，待确认）。

都没奖励诚实的“不确定”

原文 τ-bench 的 0/1 判分如上。已有研究 Kalai et al. 2025 认为打分方式奖励猜测、不奖励弃答（待确认）。

推测 MT-Bench 的冗长偏差可能让“承认不确定”的短回答吃亏，论文未测。

知识是静态的

原文 τ-bench 用固定数据库；MemGPT 用固定聊天记录和 2018 年 Wikipedia 快照；Reflexion 的题目都有标准答案。

推测 没测过 runbook 过期、两份 policy 互相矛盾。

没有权限边界

原文 MemGPT 一个 agent 服务一个用户；τ-bench 每个对话只服务一个用户。

推测 企业里同一个 agent 的记忆和工具横跨不同团队和权限，谁能查到什么没有被测过。

推测 依据：pass@k 与 pass^k 的定义（τ-bench §3 原文）

发散用 pass@k，收敛用 pass^k

分界线不在领域之间，而在同一个 workflow 的不同步骤之间，和设计里的 double diamond 是同一个结构。

发散探索方案、提出假设

生成很多，人来挑，对应 pass@k。失败的样子：假发散，同一个想法换三种配色。

交接锁定方案

要带过去：选了什么、为什么选、明确不要什么、约束。失败的样子：执行时滑回被否掉的方向。已有研究 长 context 中间的信息容易被忽略（Lost in the Middle，Liu et al. 2023）；loop engineering 资料称之为 intent debt。

收敛执行、验证假设

每次都对、做出来的就是选中的那个，对应 pass^k。失败的样子：说“做好了”，其实偏离了选择。

Reflexion 的错题本是收敛工具：在需要探索的 WebShop 上没有进步（Reflexion §4 原文）。

十七条设计原则

危险程度看可发现性，不只看失败类型

会报错 / 不报错 × 可逆 / 不可逆。最危险的是不报错、且已被拿去做决策的失败。

依据：τ-bench 0/1 判分与失败分类（§3, §5.2 原文）
对 silent 的失败，把证据摆出来

展示实际执行的 query 和数据范围；batch 操作给覆盖率，不要只给一个 Done。

依据：τ-bench“复合请求做一半”占 19%（§5.2 原文）
写操作前，结构化确认关键参数

列出型号、金额、ID、旧值→新值，比让 agent 复述一段话更能拦住错误。

依据：τ-bench 参数或信息错占失败约 55%（§5.2 原文）
审批要分级，并且要让批准有意义

按风险分只读、可逆写、不可逆写。每步都批准会变成橡皮图章，出事时责任落在点按钮的人身上。

依据：τ-bench 判分不看过程（§3 原文）；moral crumple zone（Elish，已有研究）
先让人独立判断，再揭示 AI 的意见

AI 建议出现的时机，决定人的判断还独不独立。

依据：MT-Bench 分歧案例中 34% 的人改了判断（§4 原文）；anchoring、automation bias（已有研究）
LLM judge 筛明显的，接近平局的交给人

被排除的难判题，恰恰最需要人的判断。

依据：85% 一致率是排除平局后的 S2，计入平局是 66%（MT-Bench §4 原文）
用反常识的规则测 agent 是否真的在守 SOP

demo 好看，可能只是因为任务碰巧符合常识。

依据：retail 拿掉 policy 只降 4.4 分，airline 降 22.4 分（τ-bench Table 3 原文）
交接点要显式化

把“锁定方案”做成一步：agent 先复述选定方案、理由、不要的东西和约束，人核对后存成 brief。

依据：Lost in the Middle（已有研究）；intent debt（loop engineering 资料）
明确“谁的品味”定义了好回答

通用模型默认反映一小群标注员的偏好。用 system prompt、领域示例或自己用户的反馈，把“好”的定义换成你的用户。

依据：InstructGPT §5.2“Who are we aligning to?”（原文）
给 agent 一个可以安全犯错的沙盒

风险高不等于不能试错，而是不能在真实环境里试错。dry-run、测试环境、样本数据验证让 agent 在沙盒里反复试，提交只有一次。人的位置从“盯每一次尝试”换成“定义什么算成功、看最终提交”。紧急事故的瓶颈是时间。

依据：Reflexion 先自测再提交（§3–4 原文）；τ-bench“直接面对用户只有一次机会”（§5 原文）
让 agent 学到的东西可查看、可编辑

让用户或管理员能审核、修改、删除 agent 记住的教训，像管理一份共享 SOP。

依据：Reflexion 反思可读（§6 原文）、Evaluator 会误判（§4 原文）；CLEA 征稿的 memory 与 alignment drift（原文）
让“不确定”在界面上有位置

区分查到的和推断的，展示依据，置信度低时主动建议核实，并且不惩罚模型说“不知道”。

依据：InstructGPT 仍会犯简单错误（§4.3 原文）；Kalai et al. 2025（已有研究，待确认）
记忆和文档要带来源、时间、负责人，冲突要摆出来

引用 runbook 或 policy 时说明出处和更新日期；两份说法矛盾时让用户看到，而不是默默选一个。

依据：MemGPT 记忆没有来源和冲突规则、测试材料全是静态的（§2–3 原文）；τ-bench 把冲突事实列为挑战（§6 原文）
给记忆一份可见的目录

标明柜子里有什么、多新、哪里有冲突。agent 不再只能“凭感觉搜”，人也知道它记住了什么。

依据：MemGPT 检索有局限、翻页常提前停（§3.2 原文）
不把解释当证据，用反事实测试审计

流畅的推理是表达，不是验证。高风险结论要能指回具体的查询结果；审计时换顺序、删证据、去掉用户表态，看结论会不会变。

依据：ReAct 中 CoT 有 14% 答对但推理错（Table 2 原文）；MT-Bench 位置偏差与交换测试（§3.3 原文）；Turpin et al. 2023 等（已有研究）
置信度要在自己的任务上校准，优先展示“区分”而不是精确数字

RLHF 模型的概率不能直接当置信度。用真实工单和查询重新校准；界面上用“高 / 中 / 低”把需要复核的挑出来，往往比“73%”更有用。

依据：RLHF 模型很不校准、调温度可修补，陌生任务上校准变差但区分能力能迁移（Calibration 原文）
每个指标都配一个“会被怎么钻空子”的检查

指标一旦被当成目标，就会被钻空子（Goodhart's law）。任何用来训练或评估 agent 的指标，都配一个反事实测试或交叉检查。

依据：MT-Bench 冗长偏差（§3.3 原文）、InstructGPT KL penalty（§3.5 原文）、Reflexion 自测误判（§4 原文）；Goodhart 1975、Strathern 1997（已有研究）

Evaluation of Interactive Agents

区分安静失败
Your benchmark scores outcomes. How do you distinguish failures the user would notice from failures that look like success?
追问参照物
What's the human baseline on this task, and how did you measure it?
奖励诚实的放弃

Can We Trust the Judge?

“人类”是谁
Agreement with which humans? How does judge–human agreement change when the raters are domain experts rather than generalists?
共享偏差
When the judge and humans agree, how do you know they aren't sharing the same bias, for example toward fluent, confident answers?
模拟用户可不可信
How do you validate that your simulated users behave like real users, especially ones who aren't sure what they want?
理由能不能当审计依据
If a judge's written rationale doesn't reflect what actually drove its score, how should we audit LLM judges: by reading rationales, or only through counterfactual tests?
置信度在我们的领域准不准
After post-training, how do you recalibrate a model's confidence for a specific enterprise domain, and how much labeled data does that take?

Human-AI Coevolution

被说对还是被说服
When people revise their judgment after seeing the model's reasoning, how do you tell whether they were corrected or just persuaded?
审批疲劳
As approvals become routine, how do you keep human oversight meaningful instead of a rubber stamp?
谁的偏好
Preference data comes from a small group of raters but shapes how millions of people work. How do you account for users whose preferences aren't in that group?

Continual Learning for Enterprise AI Agents

规则变了怎么办
When a company's policies change, how do you re-validate the agent, and who updates the ground truth it's evaluated against?
谁审核学到的教训
When an agent learns from its own reflections, who reviews what it learned? How do you catch a wrong lesson before it spreads to future tasks?
能不能跨任务迁移
Reflexion improves retries on the same task. What evidence do we have that lessons transfer across tasks in an enterprise setting?
学新忘旧
InstructGPT mixed in pretraining data to limit the alignment tax. What's the equivalent safeguard when an enterprise agent keeps learning from its own users?
知识过期和冲突
Your memory benchmarks use static data. How should an agent handle runbooks that go stale or policies that contradict each other, and how does it tell the user?

τ-bench

τ-bench: A Benchmark for Tool-Agent-User Interaction in Real-World Domains · Yao, Shinn, Razavi, Narasimhan · Sierra · 2024

一场模拟客服上岗考试：agent 要一边跟用户对话、一边调工具、一边守业务规则。

arXiv 2406.12045 ↗HTML 全文 ↗Evaluation of Interactive Agents
四句话论文卡
主张

真实部署要求 agent 同时跟人对话、调工具、守规则；现有 agent 在这种设定下平均成功率低，而且很不稳定。

最强证据

gpt-4o 在 retail 的 pass^1 超过 60%，pass^8 低于 25%；规则反常识的 airline 场景里，拿掉 policy 后分数大幅下降。原文 §5

边界

模拟用户、只有客服场景、成败二元判分、没有 human baseline。

产品还要验证

失败能否被发现和恢复、相对人类的基准、模糊请求的处理能力。

和 Transformer 那篇的对照

Transformer 的证据是 BLEU：输出和参考答案有多像。τ-bench 的证据是数据库最终状态：事情到底办成没有。

核心指标

pass^1 和 pass^k

咖啡师类比：pass^1 是随便点一杯拿铁、这杯做对的概率；pass^8 是连续 8 天点同一杯、8 天都做对的概率。

56%pass^1：所有尝试的平均成功率
25%pass^8

计算器里的 4 个任务是教学示例，不是论文数据。

  • 原文 §3 8 次是完全独立的重跑：同一份任务剧本，从零开始，没有记忆，也不会被纠正。
  • 原文 §5 agent 的 temperature 是 0.0，模拟用户是 1.0。不稳定主要来自用户换了说法。
  • 原文 §3 pass@k 是 k 次至少一次成功，pass^k 要求全部成功。k 越大，前者越高、后者越低。
  • 原文 §5.1 retail 全部任务跑一轮约 200 美元。推测 所以 k 取到 8 主要受成本限制，不是行业标准。产品的 k 应由真实使用频率倒推。

两套考题，三种裁判方式

Pairwise comparison给两个回答，选更好的或判平局
Single answer grading只看一个回答，直接打分
Reference-guided grading先给参考答案再评，用于数学等有对错的题

pretraining 学的是“续写得像”，不是“照用户意图做”，模型变大解决不了。用人类示范加排序做 post-training，可以把模型对齐成助手。原文 §1

Problem · §1

续写不等于回答

原文 对只做过 pretraining 的模型说“用 6 岁小孩能听懂的话解释登月”，它可能接着写出一串类似的题目，因为网上这句话后面常跟着列表。作者把模型编造事实、输出有害内容、答非所问统称为 misalignment，并指出模型变大不能解决。

Method · §3

三步法，像带实习生

带实习生InstructGPT规模
给参考文档Pretraining（直接用 GPT-3）
让他看我怎么做SFT：标注员亲手写好回答，模型照着学~13k prompt
他上手，我当 mentorReward model：标注员给多个回答排序，训练一个模型模仿他们的品味~33k prompt
PPO：模型大量练习，由 reward model 打分~31k prompt
直接交给他做部署 / inference

谁在变，谁冻结

模型在 Step 3 的角色会变吗
SFT 模型参照物：KL penalty 拿它比，看跑偏多远（实习生第一周的录像）冻结
Reward model考官：给每次练习打分冻结

得分 = 考官打分 − 跟 SFT 模型差了多远。防的是 reward hacking：为了讨好考官变成另一个人。原文 §3.5

PPO-ptx

和 MT-Bench 的关系

reward model 本质上就是一个 LLM-as-a-judge。MT-Bench 用裁判来评估，这里用裁判来训练。

我们的讨论

Hallucination 和 sycophancy 是两个问题

Hallucination（编造）Sycophancy（谄媚）
表现自信地说出不存在的事实、引用顺着用户说，一被质疑就改口
主要根源Pretraining：目标是“续写得像”RLHF：标注员偏爱让自己舒服的回答
RLHF 的作用可能放大也可能缓解（本篇 TruthfulQA 改善）主要来源之一
  • 已有研究 Kalai et al. 2025 用考试类比：空着不写得 0 分、乱猜还有机会，学生学会猜；打分方式奖励猜测是 hallucination 持续的原因之一（待确认）。
  • 推测 不存在“纯净”的模型：只做 pretraining 带的是互联网的偏见，加了 RLHF 带的是标注员的偏好。更有用的问题是谁的影响、是否说清、能否被你的用户纠正。
  • 推测 通用模型的“好回答”反映标注员品味；凌晨三点处理故障的 SRE 可能只想要一条能直接跑的 query。

Reflexion

Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning · Shinn, Cassano, Berman, Gopinath, Narasimhan, Yao · 2023

不改模型参数，把失败写成文字反思存进记忆，重做时参考。像写错题本。

arXiv 2303.11366 ↗Continual Learning for Enterprise AI Agents
四句话论文卡
主张

agent 不用改模型参数，只靠把失败写成文字反思、存进记忆，就能在重做时变好。

我们的讨论

风险高不等于不能试错，而是不能在真实环境里试错

  • 推测 能不能用错题本，取决于有没有一个失败不要钱、又能可靠判对错的沙盒。Infrastructure 可以先 dry-run，data pipeline 可以先用样本验证；紧急事故的瓶颈是时间。
  • 推测 低风险的 chatbot 未必能接受 12 次重试：每次失败用户都看得见，信任会先耗光。
  • 推测 人的位置从“盯每一次尝试”换成“定义什么算成功、搭沙盒、看最终提交”。评估器定错了，agent 越自主，越会自信地收敛到错误答案。
已有研究 和 loop engineering 的关系

Loop engineering 通常被描述为 Goal → Action → Observation → Adjustment，再加持久记忆（spine），和 Actor、Evaluator、Self-Reflection、长期记忆一一对应。但 loop 不一定包含反思：逐个处理一批对象、或单纯重试到通过，都不需要写教训。相关资料列出的风险包括 intent debt、comprehension debt、cognitive surrender。

MemGPT

MemGPT: Towards LLMs as Operating Systems · Packer, Wooders, Lin, Fang, Patil, Stoica, Gonzalez · UC Berkeley · 2023

借鉴操作系统的虚拟内存：context 是一张小书桌，外部存储是大档案柜，由 LLM 自己决定什么放桌上、什么收进柜子、什么时候去翻。

arXiv 2310.08560 ↗Continual Learning for Enterprise AI Agents
四句话论文卡
主张

借鉴操作系统的虚拟内存，让 LLM 自己管理“桌上”和“柜子里”的记忆，就能突破 context 长度的限制。原文 摘要, §2

最强证据

深度记忆检索准确率：GPT-4 从 32.1% 到 92.5%，GPT-4 Turbo 从 35.3% 到 93.4%；嵌套查找 4 层仍稳定，基线第 3 层降到 0%。原文 §3

边界

需要强的函数调用能力（GPT-3.5 带不动）；检索不全、翻页提前停；基线只看有损摘要，对比不完全对等；准确率由 GPT-4 裁判判定；测试材料全是静态的。

知识过期和冲突怎么处理、记忆的来源与权限、agent 自己选择保留什么由谁审核。

Problem · 摘要, §1

Context 有限，但有两类任务需要很多记忆

  • 原文 分析比 context 还长的文档。
  • 原文 多次会话的长期对话：agent 要在很多次对话之间记住用户，并随交流不断更新对用户的了解。
操作系统类比

电脑的内存小而快，硬盘大而慢；操作系统决定什么放内存、什么换到硬盘，让程序感觉自己有很大的内存（virtual memory）。MemGPT 把 context window 当内存、外部存储当硬盘，让 LLM 自己当操作系统。

Method · §2

书桌和档案柜

位置名称放什么谁能改

Archival storage任意长期信息，比如文档agent 读写

  • 原文 §2 Self-directed：agent 自己调用函数决定存什么、查什么，不需要用户介入。
  • 原文 §2 Heartbeat：agent 可以连续调用多个函数（查、翻页、再查），做完再回复用户。

你的预测论文里的对应

ReAct

ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models · Yao, Zhao, Yu, Du, Shafran, Narasimhan, Cao · Princeton / Google · ICLR 2023

让模型交替“想”和“做”：Thought → Action → Observation → Thought……我们读过的 agent 论文里的循环，大多都从这里来。

四句话论文卡
主张

把推理和行动交替起来：想帮助做（规划、调整、处理意外），做帮助想（拿到外部真实信息、减少编造），而且过程人能读懂。原文 摘要

Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know

Kadavath, Conerly, Askell, … Olah, Kaplan · Anthropic · 2022

模型知不知道自己不知道？测三件事：给选项的概率准不准、能不能判断自己答案的对错、能不能在回答前预测自己会不会。

arXiv 2207.05221 ↗Can We Trust the Judge?Human-AI Coevolution
四句话论文卡
主张

语言模型在很大程度上知道自己知道什么：大模型在格式合适的题目上校准良好，能评估自己答案的对错，还能预测自己会不会答对。原文 摘要

最强证据

520 亿模型在选择题和判断题上校准良好；P(IK) 在 TriviaQA 上 AUROC 0.864；给相关文章时 P(IK) 上升，给错误提示时下降。原文

边界

RLHF 模型很不校准，需调温度修补；对格式敏感（“以上都不是”会破坏校准）；陌生任务上信心不足；P(IK) 需要训练模型内部；只测了有标准答案的任务。

产品还要验证

在自己的任务上重新校准；开放式任务如何估计把握；置信度怎么展示，才能让人做出更好的决定。

基础概念

校准、区分能力、模型大小

校准（calibration）

天气预报说“70% 下雨”的那些日子，约 70% 真的下雨，就是校准良好。衡量的是信心和实际表现是否匹配，不等于准确。

区分能力（discrimination）

某城市 30% 的日子下雨，每天都报“30%”的预报员完全校准，却毫无用处。还要能把会对的和会错的分开，用 AUROC 衡量：0.5 是瞎猜，1.0 是完美。

模型“大”指什么

通常指参数多：可调的“旋钮”数量，代表容量。训练数据量是另一回事。本篇四个模型（8 亿到 520 亿）数据都是 8500 亿 token，只改参数。

规模定律

已有研究 Kaplan et al. 2020：表现随参数、数据、算力可预测地提升。Hoffmann et al. 2022（Chinchilla）：参数和数据要同步加，约每个参数 20 个 token。

问题名称考试类比

  • 已有研究 这测的就是考试里的元认知（metacognition）：好学生不一定每题都会，但通常知道自己哪题不会。
  • 推测 调温度只改变概率的整体尖平，不改变信心的高低顺序；它能修好校准，说明 RLHF 可能没破坏模型对“自己知道什么”的判断，只是让表达更夸张。
  • 人类反馈是“过度自信”和“谄媚”的重要来源，但不是幻觉（主要来自 pretraining）和偏见（RLHF 后无显著改善，InstructGPT §4.2）的主要源头。问题不在“人”，在优化目标：喜欢不等于正确。
  • RLHF 仍被追捧，因为：没有它模型基本没法直接用（InstructGPT 13 亿胜 1750 亿）；它能优化写不成规则的东西；排序数据便宜；副作用已知且可缓解（KL penalty、调温度、改标注规则）；配方在演变，偏好打分逐渐收缩到没有标准答案的任务。类比：用户满意度调研有偏差，但没人因此放弃，而是结合行为数据交叉验证。
推测 设计含义

不能直接把 RLHF 模型的概率当置信度展示，要在自己的任务上重新校准（用真实工单和查询）。陌生领域偏保守是相对安全的失败方式，但保守过度会回到审批疲劳。区分能力比精确数字更有用：用“高 / 中 / 低”把需要复核的挑出来，可能就够了。

模型是怎么学习的

Loss、梯度下降、训练 / 验证 / 测试集、过拟合、参数与规模

机器学习最底层的几个概念。现场研究者张口就是“loss 降了”“过拟合了”，这一课把它们讲清楚。

一句话版本
Loss

给“错得有多离谱”打分，越低越好。训练就是想办法让它变低。

梯度下降

在大雾里下山：感受脚下往哪边斜，往下迈一小步，再感受，再迈。

训练 / 验证 / 测试集

练习题、模拟考、正式考试。正式考试的题绝对不能混进练习题。

过拟合

背答案而不是学会：练习题满分，换道新题就不会。

1 · Loss

给“错得有多离谱”打分

LLM 的 pretraining 是在玩“猜下一个词”：读到“今天天气很”，模型给每个可能的下一个 token 一个概率。实际下一个词是“热”，模型给“热”的概率越高，loss 越低。

这个打分方式叫 cross-entropy（交叉熵），它对“自信地猜错”罚得特别重。细节见概念课 打分方式决定模型。

4 · 过拟合与泛化

是学会了，还是背下来了？

  • Overfitting（过拟合）：在训练数据上很好，在没见过的数据上很差。
  • Generalization（泛化）：能做好没见过的题。
  • 怎么发现：训练集 loss 一直降，验证集 loss 开始上升，就说明开始背答案了。
这个问题贯穿了好几篇

Benchmark 饱和、数据污染（Reflexion），Calibration 里 P(IK) 迁移到新任务时校准变差，问的都是同一件事：它是学会了，还是背下来了？

打分方式决定模型

Cross-entropy · Proper scoring rule · Goodhart's law · KL penalty

用什么方式打分，模型就会变成什么样。这一课把前面几篇反复出现的现象串起来：为什么预训练模型诚实，RLHF 模型自信，只看对错的模型爱猜。

1 · Cross-entropy loss

交叉熵：重罚“自信地错”

Definition

Cross-entropy loss = −log(probability the model assigned to the correct answer). 别名：log loss、negative log-likelihood (NLL)。

给正确答案的概率Loss = −ln(p)说明
0.90.11自信且对，几乎不罚
0.50.69拿不准
0.12.30基本没想到
0.014.61几乎确定不是它，结果错了，重罚

Perplexity（困惑度）：e 的平均 loss 次方，直觉上是“模型平均在几个选项之间犹豫”。越低越好。

2 · Proper scoring rule

它两个方向都罚，所以奖励说真话

已有研究 Definition

“A scoring rule is proper if a forecaster maximizes their expected score by reporting their true beliefs.” 只有如实报告，长期得分才最高。

真实下雨概率 70%，预报员报长期平均 loss
50%（过度保守）0.69
70%（如实）0.61
90%（过度自信）0.77
  • 这解释了 Calibration 的核心发现：预训练目标本身奖励诚实的概率，所以预训练模型校准好；RLHF 换成“人更喜欢哪个”，不罚说错概率，所以变差。
  • 原文 Calibration 陌生任务上的信心不足出现在另外训练的 P(IK) 输出头上，更像单一训练数据导致的迁移问题。推测 交叉熵让模型诚实而不是胆小；保守更可能来自“认不出自己会”。
  • Brier score 也是一种 proper scoring rule（Calibration 里 P(IK) 的 Brier 是 0.151）。
3 · Goodhart's law

当指标变成目标

原始版本 · Charles Goodhart, 1975

“Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes.”

流行版本 · Marilyn Strathern, 1997

“When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.”

指标之所以有用，是因为它和真正关心的东西碰巧相关。一旦直接优化它，就会找到“指标高、真正关心的不高”的捷径，相关性就断了。近亲：Campbell's law（1976）。

AI 里的名字English
Reward hackingThe agent exploits flaws in the reward function to get high reward without doing the intended task.
Specification gamingSatisfying the literal specification of an objective without achieving the intended outcome.
Reward model overoptimizationOptimizing too hard against a learned reward model eventually decreases true quality.
  • 已有研究 OpenAI 2016 划船游戏 CoastRunners：agent 原地绕圈撞得分道具，就是不去终点。
  • 已有研究 Gao, Schulman & Hilton 2022：对着 reward model 优化时，真实质量先升后降，呈倒 U 形（待确认）。

5 · 复习 KL penalty

KL penalty：给 Goodhart 拴的一根绳

KL divergence · Definition

“A measure of how much one probability distribution differs from a reference distribution.” 两个概率分布差多远。它和交叉熵是亲戚：KL = 交叉熵 − 熵，可以理解为“用另一个分布来猜，比用真实分布猜，多出来的那部分 loss”。

  • 原文 InstructGPT §3.5 RLHF 第三步的得分 = reward model 打分 − β × KL(当前模型 ‖ SFT 模型)。比较的是同一个 prompt 下，两个模型对下一个 token 给出的概率分布。
  • 防的是什么：reward model 只是近似人类品味，有漏洞。不加约束，模型会钻漏洞拿高分，正是 reward model overoptimization。
  • β 是绳子的长短：β 大，模型几乎不敢改变，学不到东西；β 小，模型可以跑很远，也更容易钻空子。
  • 类比：实习生可以按 mentor 的喜好调整风格，但每次都要拿第一周的录像来对照，偏离太远就扣分。
  • 已有研究 DPO 虽然省掉了 reward model，数学上仍隐含一个相对参照模型的 KL 约束。
推测 带回产品

每个指标都配一个“会被怎么钻空子”的检查

  • 你熟悉的版本：客服以“平均结单时间”为 KPI，工单被草草关闭；推荐系统只优化点击率，出现标题党。
  • 任何用来训练或评估 agent 的指标，都要问：它最容易被怎样钻空子？再配一个反事实测试或交叉检查。

Reading the papers together

Shared blind spots

The facts in each item come from the papers; only the last line, what it means for products, is inference.

LLMs stand in for people

Paper τ-bench uses gpt-4 as the simulated user; MT-Bench uses GPT-4 as judge; InstructGPT uses a reward model in place of labelers; Reflexion uses an LLM or self-written tests as its Evaluator on some tasks.

Our inference None validated against enterprise domain experts.

“Humans” are a narrow group

Paper MT-Bench: grad students and crowd users. InstructGPT: about 40 labelers mostly in the US or Southeast Asia. τ-bench: no human baseline reported.

Our inference Agreeing with them isn't agreeing with SREs or admins.

Humans disagree with each other

Paper MT-Bench expert agreement 81%; InstructGPT labeler agreement about 73–77%. Disagreement gets averaged away in training and scoring.

Our inference Minority user groups' preferences are the first to disappear.

Outcome only, not process

Paper τ-bench authors state r=1 is necessary but not sufficient: acting without confirmation still counts as success. MT-Bench scores only the final answer.

Our inference Enterprises need to know who approved and what the agent saw.

All failures weigh the same

Paper τ-bench scores 0 or 1. Refusing when the rules require it scores, but when the task was doable, “escalate to a human” and “do it wrong” both score 0.

Our inference Can't tell noticeable failures from silent ones.

Preferred isn't correct

Paper MT-Bench judges favor longer answers and are misled by wrong math; Reflexion's self-written tests pass wrong code (16% on Rust).

Our inference A polished but wrong RCA may score well.

Evaluation and training get gamed

Paper InstructGPT adds a KL penalty specifically to stop exploiting the reward model.

Prior research Reward hacking is well known; RLHF models tend toward sycophancy (Anthropic 2023, to verify).

No reward for honest uncertainty

Paper τ-bench 0/1 scoring as above. Prior research Kalai et al. 2025 argue that scoring which rewards guessing over abstaining keeps hallucination alive (to verify).

Our inference MT-Bench's verbosity bias may penalize short “I'm not sure” answers; untested.

Knowledge is static

Paper τ-bench uses a fixed database; MemGPT uses fixed chat logs and a 2018 Wikipedia dump; Reflexion's tasks all have answer keys.

Our inference Stale runbooks and contradicting policies were never tested.

No access boundaries

Paper MemGPT pairs one agent with one user; τ-bench serves one user per conversation.

Our inference In enterprises, one agent's memory and tools span teams and permission levels, and who can retrieve what was never tested.

Our inference Based on the pass@k and pass^k definitions (τ-bench §3 Paper)

Diverge with pass@k, converge with pass^k

The line isn't between domains but between steps of the same workflow. It's the same shape as the design double diamond.

DivergeExplore directions, form hypotheses

Generate many, a person picks: pass@k. Failure looks like fake divergence: one idea in three color schemes.

Hand-offLock the direction

Carry over what was chosen, why, what's ruled out, and the constraints. Failure looks like drifting back to rejected directions. Prior research Information in the middle of long contexts gets ignored (Lost in the Middle, Liu et al. 2023); loop engineering writers call this intent debt.

ConvergeExecute, verify

Right every time, and it's what was chosen: pass^k. Failure looks like “done” while quietly deviating.

Reflexion's mistake notebook is a converging tool: it made no progress on exploration-heavy WebShop (Reflexion §4 Paper).

List the model, amount, IDs, old → new values. This catches more errors than having the agent paraphrase.

Basis: wrong arguments or info are about 55% of τ-bench failures (§5.2 Paper)
Tier approvals and make them meaningful

Split by risk: read-only, reversible write, irreversible write. Approving every step becomes a rubber stamp, and blame lands on whoever clicked.

Basis: τ-bench scores outcome, not process (§3 Paper); moral crumple zone (Elish, Prior research)
Let people judge first, then reveal the AI's view

When the AI's suggestion appears decides whether human judgment stays independent.

Basis: 34% of people revised their judgment in disagreement cases (MT-Bench §4 Paper); anchoring, automation bias (Prior research)
LLM judges screen the obvious; humans take the close calls

The hard cases left out of the headline number are exactly the ones needing human judgment.

Basis: 85% agreement is S2 without ties; with ties it's 66% (MT-Bench §4 Paper)
Test SOP adherence with counter-intuitive rules

A good demo may only mean the tasks happened to match common sense.

Basis: removing the policy cost 4.4 points in retail but 22.4 in airline (τ-bench Table 3 Paper)
Make the hand-off explicit

Make “lock the direction” a step: the agent restates the chosen option, the reasons, what's ruled out and the constraints; a person checks it and saves it as a brief.

Basis: Lost in the Middle (Prior research); intent debt (loop engineering writing)
Be explicit about whose taste defines “good”

General models reflect a small group of labelers by default. Use system prompts, domain examples or your own users' feedback to redefine “good” for your users.

Basis: InstructGPT §5.2 “Who are we aligning to?” (Paper)
Give agents a sandbox to fail safely

High risk doesn't rule out trial and error; it rules out trial and error in production. Dry runs, test environments and sample-data validation let the agent iterate, then submit once. People move from watching every attempt to defining success and checking the final submission. In urgent incidents the bottleneck is time.

Basis: Reflexion self-tests before submitting (§3–4 Paper); τ-bench “a user-facing agent gets one chance” (§5 Paper)
Make what agents learn viewable and editable

Let users or admins review, edit and delete the lessons an agent keeps, like a shared SOP.

Basis: Reflexion reflections are readable (§6 Paper), evaluators misjudge (§4 Paper); CLEA's memory and alignment drift topics (Paper)
Give uncertainty a place in the interface

Separate what was looked up from what was inferred, show the basis, suggest verification when confidence is low, and don't penalize “I don't know”.

Basis: InstructGPT still makes simple mistakes (§4.3 Paper); Kalai et al. 2025 (Prior research, to verify)
Give memories and documents a source, date and owner; surface conflicts

When citing a runbook or policy, show where it came from and when it was updated; when two disagree, show the user instead of silently picking one.

Basis: MemGPT memories have no provenance or conflict rules, and its test data is static (§2–3 Paper); τ-bench lists conflicting facts as a challenge (§6 Paper)
Give memory a visible directory

Show what's in the cabinet, how fresh it is and where it conflicts, so the agent isn't searching blind and people know what it remembers.

Basis: MemGPT retrieval has limits and often stops paging early (§3.2 Paper)
Don't treat explanations as evidence; audit with counterfactuals

Fluent reasoning is expression, not verification. High-stakes conclusions should point back to specific lookups; audits should reorder inputs, remove evidence or drop the user's stated view and see if the conclusion moves.

Basis: 14% of CoT answers were right with wrong reasoning (ReAct Table 2 Paper); MT-Bench position bias and swap test (§3.3 Paper); Turpin et al. 2023 and others (Prior research)

An RLHF model's probabilities aren't confidence as-is. Recalibrate on real tickets and queries; in the interface, high / medium / low that flags what needs review often beats “73%”.

Basis: RLHF models are badly calibrated and fixable with temperature; on unfamiliar tasks calibration degrades while discrimination transfers (Calibration Paper)
Pair every metric with a “how could this be gamed?” check

Once a metric becomes a target it gets gamed (Goodhart's law). Pair any metric used to train or evaluate an agent with a counterfactual test or cross-check.

Attention Is All You Need

Introduces the Transformer: an encoder–decoder built around attention, with no recurrence or convolution, for machine translation. Later large models reused and reshaped its components.

arXiv 1706.03762 ↗Official PDF ↗

Claim

τ-bench

τ-bench: A Benchmark for Tool-Agent-User Interaction in Real-World Domains · Yao, Shinn, Razavi, Narasimhan · Sierra · 2024

A simulated customer-service job test: the agent has to talk to a user, call tools and follow business rules at the same time.

arXiv 2406.12045 ↗Full HTML ↗Evaluation of Interactive Agents
The paper in four lines
Claim

Real deployment requires talking to people, calling tools and following rules at once; current agents succeed rarely in this setting and are very inconsistent.

Strongest evidence

gpt-4o's retail pass^1 is above 60% but pass^8 is under 25%; in the counter-intuitive airline domain, removing the policy drops scores sharply. Paper §5

Limits

Simulated users, customer service only, binary scoring, no human baseline.

Still to validate for products

Whether failures can be noticed and recovered from, the human baseline, and handling of vague requests.

Compared with the Transformer paper

The Transformer's evidence is BLEU: how close the output is to a reference. τ-bench's evidence is the final database state: whether the job actually got done.

Core metric

pass^1 and pass^k

Barista analogy: pass^1 is the chance one latte comes out right; pass^8 is the chance the same latte comes out right on all 8 days you order it.

56%pass^1: average success over all attempts
25%pass^8

The four tasks in the calculator are a teaching example, not paper data.

  • Paper §3 The 8 runs are fully independent: same scenario, from scratch, no memory, no correction.
  • Paper §5 The agent runs at temperature 0.0 and the simulated user at 1.0. Most of the inconsistency comes from users phrasing things differently.
  • Paper §3 pass@k means at least one of k succeeds; pass^k means all k do. As k grows, the first rises and the second falls.
  • Paper §5.1 One pass over all retail tasks costs about $200. Our inference So k=8 is mostly a cost limit, not a standard. A product's k should come from real usage frequency.

Basics

Calibration, discrimination, model size

Calibration

If it rains on about 70% of the days a forecaster says “70%”, the forecaster is well calibrated. It measures whether confidence matches performance; it isn't accuracy.

Discrimination

In a city where it rains 30% of days, a forecaster who always says “30%” is perfectly calibrated and useless. You also need to separate right from wrong cases, measured by AUROC: 0.5 is chance, 1.0 is perfect.

What “bigger” means

Usually more parameters: the number of adjustable “knobs”, a measure of capacity. Training data is separate. Here all four models (800M to 52B) saw the same 850B tokens; only parameters vary.

Scaling laws

Prior research Kaplan et al. 2020: performance improves predictably with parameters, data and compute. Hoffmann et al. 2022 (Chinchilla): grow parameters and data together, about 20 tokens per parameter.

Problem · Abstract, §1

Three questions, from easy to hard

QuestionNameExam analogy
On multiple choice, are the option probabilities accurate?CalibrationHow sure you are of each option
After answering, can it tell whether that answer is right?P(True)Checking before you hand in: did I get this one?
Before answering, can it predict whether it will be right?P(IK), I KnowSeeing a question and knowing whether you can do it

These are three different probabilities; each can be checked for calibration and discrimination on its own. Calibration doesn't require getting all three right.

Bigger is better calibrated, but RLHF makes it worse

  • Paper On multiple choice, larger models and more examples mean better calibration; lettered options help; in true/false format the 52B model is very well calibrated.

RLHF makes answers more preferred and more truthful (InstructGPT roughly doubled TruthfulQA), but it optimizes for which answer people prefer, not whether probabilities are accurate. Raters favor decisive answers, so the model learns to push probabilities to extremes.

Discrimination matters more than exact numbers: high / medium / low may be enough to flag what needs review.

The most basic ideas in machine learning. Researchers say “the loss went down” or “it overfit” without explanation; this lesson makes them clear.

1 · Loss

Scoring how wrong the model is

LLM pretraining is a next-word guessing game: after “the weather today is very”, the model assigns a probability to every possible next token. If the actual next word is “hot”, the more probability it gave “hot”, the lower the loss.

This scoring is called cross-entropy, and it punishes confident mistakes especially hard. Details in the concept lesson Scoring shapes the model.

4 · Overfitting and generalization

Did it learn, or did it memorize?

Scoring shapes the model

Cross-entropy · Proper scoring rule · Goodhart's law · KL penalty

How you score a model decides what it becomes. This lesson ties together patterns from earlier papers: why pretrained models are honest, RLHF models are confident, and accuracy-only models guess.

1 · Cross-entropy loss

Cross-entropy punishes confident mistakes

Definition

Cross-entropy loss = −log(probability the model assigned to the correct answer). Also called log loss or negative log-likelihood (NLL).

Probability on the correct answerLoss = −ln(p)Meaning
0.90.11Confident and right, barely penalized
0.50.69Unsure
0.12.30Didn't see it coming
0.014.61Nearly ruled it out and was wrong: heavy penalty

Perplexity: e raised to the average loss; intuitively, how many options the model is torn between on average. Lower is better.

2 · Proper scoring rule

It penalizes both directions, so it rewards honesty

Prior research Definition

“A scoring rule is proper if a forecaster maximizes their expected score by reporting their true beliefs.”

True chance of rain 70%; forecaster reportsAverage loss over time
50% (underconfident)0.69
70% (honest)0.61
90% (overconfident)0.77
  • This explains Calibration's core finding: the pretraining objective rewards honest probabilities, so pretrained models are calibrated; RLHF switches to “which answer do people prefer”, which doesn't penalize miscalibration.
  • Paper Calibration Underconfidence on unfamiliar tasks shows up in the separately trained P(IK) head, more like a transfer problem from narrow training data. Our inference Cross-entropy makes models honest, not timid; caution more likely comes from not recognizing what it knows.
  • The Brier score is another proper scoring rule (P(IK) Brier 0.151 in Calibration).
3 · Goodhart's law

When a measure becomes a target

Original · Charles Goodhart, 1975

“Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes.”

Popular · Marilyn Strathern, 1997

“When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.”

A metric is useful because it happens to correlate with what you care about. Optimize it directly and you find shortcuts where the metric is high and the real goal isn't, breaking the correlation. A close relative: Campbell's law (1976).

Name in AIMeaning
Reward hackingThe agent exploits flaws in the reward function to get high reward without doing the intended task.
Specification gamingSatisfying the literal specification of an objective without achieving the intended outcome.
Reward model overoptimizationOptimizing too hard against a learned reward model eventually decreases true quality.
  • Prior research OpenAI 2016, CoastRunners: the boat circles hitting point targets instead of finishing the race.
  • Prior research Gao, Schulman & Hilton 2022: optimizing against a reward model, true quality rises then falls in an inverted U (to verify).

5 · KL penalty, revisited

KL penalty: a leash against Goodhart

KL divergence · Definition

“A measure of how much one probability distribution differs from a reference distribution.” It's a relative of cross-entropy: KL = cross-entropy − entropy, the extra loss you pay for predicting with one distribution when another is the reference.

  • Paper InstructGPT §3.5 In RLHF step 3, score = reward model score − β × KL(current model ‖ SFT model), comparing the two models' next-token distributions on the same prompt.
  • What it guards against: the reward model only approximates human taste and has flaws. Unconstrained, the model exploits them: reward model overoptimization.
  • β is the length of the leash: large β, the model barely changes; small β, it can roam far and is easier to game the reward.
  • Analogy: a new hire can adapt to their mentor's taste, but is always compared to their week-one recording and loses points for drifting too far.
  • Prior research DPO drops the reward model but still implicitly carries a KL constraint toward a reference model.
Our inference Back to product

Pair every metric with a “how could this be gamed?” check

  • Familiar versions: support teams targeted on time-to-close close tickets prematurely; recommenders optimized for clicks produce clickbait.
  • For any metric used to train or evaluate an agent, ask how it's most easily gamed, and pair it with a counterfactual test or cross-check.
